The learning journey

From raw observations
to thoughtful discoveries.

Follow the path from foundations to applications. Open the available lab, or unfold a topic to see the ideas ahead. Explore at your own pace.

  1. 01
    Foundationsxᵢ

    Start with unlabelled data

    What can you learn when the answers have not been supplied? Explore ML basics, learning types, unlabelled data, technique families, limitations and preprocessing.

    • Learning paradigms
    • Exploration
    • Preprocessing
    What will I learn?

    Decide which measurements describe an observation and what you want to discover.

    Think about itA set of forest plots has greenness and canopy-cover measurements, but no habitat labels. What could you investigate?

    Open Discovery Lab ↗
  2. 02
    Clusteringμ

    Find your first clusters

    Watch observations join their nearest centres, then see those centres move. Experiment with how many groups to ask for.

    • K-means
    • Distance & centroids
    • Choosing k
    What will I learn?

    Connect distance, group assignments and the objective of tighter clusters.

    Think about itThe current K-means playground lets you draw points, step through calculations and compare different starts.

    Open K-means lab ↗
  3. 03
    Clusteringε

    Let shape and density guide you

    Build a hierarchy of groups or look for dense neighbourhoods. Explore why different clustering assumptions produce different answers.

    • Hierarchical clustering
    • Dendrograms
    • DBSCAN
    What will I learn?

    Distinguish centre-based, hierarchical and density-based groupings.

    Think about itTwo curved bands of observations may be hard to describe using distance to a centre. How else could you define a group?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  4. 04
    EvaluationSSE

    Question your clusters

    A colourful plot is only a starting point. Examine quality, stability, noise and the meaning of the groups you find.

    • Evaluation metrics
    • Stability
    • Segmentation
    What will I learn?

    Justify a grouping using evidence and domain knowledge.

    Think about itIf a small change to the data changes your groups, how much confidence should you place in the segmentation?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  5. 05
    Linear discoveryλ

    Find the directions that matter

    Represent many measurements using fewer informative directions. Connect variance and covariance to the geometry of PCA.

    • PCA
    • Eigenvectors
    • SVD
    What will I learn?

    Explain what a linear projection preserves and what it leaves behind.

    Think about itSeveral environmental sensors may measure closely related signals. Could a smaller set of components summarise them?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  6. 06
    Nonlinear discoveryℝⁿ → ℝ²

    See beyond straight lines

    Explore neighbourhood structure in high-dimensional data with nonlinear visualisations, and learn to read those maps critically.

    • Manifolds
    • t-SNE
    • UMAP
    What will I learn?

    Compare a nonlinear embedding with PCA without treating a 2D picture as proof.

    Think about itTwo islands on an embedding may look like distinct classes. What evidence would you need before claiming they are?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  7. 07
    Pattern discoveryΔ

    Notice the unusual

    Investigate observations that differ from the rest, from isolated points to unusual behaviour over time.

    • Isolation Forest
    • Local Outlier Factor
    • Sensor sequences
    What will I learn?

    Distinguish a meaningful anomaly from noise or a measurement problem.

    Think about itA sudden sensor spike could signal a real event or a faulty instrument. How would you investigate it?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  8. 08
    Representation learningx → z → x̂

    Learn a compact representation

    Compress observations through a bottleneck, reconstruct them and investigate what the learned representation captures.

    • Autoencoders
    • Reconstruction loss
    • VAE introduction
    What will I learn?

    Relate an encoder, a latent representation and a decoder.

    Think about itWhat happens when an autoencoder tries to reconstruct an observation unlike the examples it learned from?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  9. 09
    Self-supervisionx → ?

    Let data create the learning task

    Construct training targets from the data itself. Explore how masked and contrastive tasks can prepare useful representations.

    • Pretext tasks
    • Masked learning
    • Contrastive learning
    What will I learn?

    Explain how self-supervision differs from conventional unsupervised discovery.

    Think about itHide part of an image and predict it from the visible context. Where did the training target come from?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  10. 10
    AssociationsA ⇒ B

    Discover what happens together

    Find frequent combinations and interpret association rules using support, confidence and lift.

    • Apriori
    • FP-Growth
    • Association rules
    What will I learn?

    Describe a co-occurrence pattern without confusing it with a cause.

    Think about itTwo items often appear in the same basket. Is the relationship useful, or are both items simply very common?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  11. 11
    RecommendationsR ≈ UVᵀ

    Turn similarity into recommendations

    Connect interactions, item features and learned factors to recommendations, then examine how to evaluate them.

    • Collaborative filtering
    • Matrix factorisation
    • Evaluation
    What will I learn?

    Compare collaborative, content-based and hybrid approaches.

    Think about itRecommender systems can combine several learning approaches. What data would each approach need?

    Interactive lab in preparation
  12. 12
    Critical practice? → evidence

    Interpret, research and reflect

    Bring the methods together. Consider bias, ethics and interpretability, communicate your investigation and revisit the key ideas.

    • Ethics & bias
    • Applied research
    • Reflection
    What will I learn?

    Explain what your model supports, what it cannot establish and why the limitations matter.

    Think about itWho could be affected by a discovered grouping, and what would make your conclusions trustworthy?
